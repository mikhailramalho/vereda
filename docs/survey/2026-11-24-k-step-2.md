# A k-step of 2 under --property-file, as ESBMC's SV-COMP wrapper

Recorded 2026-11-24.

`vereda.py --property-file` with `--incremental-bmc` now passes
`--unlimited-k-steps --k-step 2`, the flags ESBMC's `esbmc-wrapper.py` uses,
instead of the step of 8 chosen in
`2026-11-23-no-largest-bound-under-incremental-bmc.md`.

That step was measured only on the 107 ReachSafety tasks that had stopped at
bound 50, where it answered 56 against 53 for a step of 2. It was not
measured on the tasks answered at small bounds, and it loses some of them:
checking bounds 1, 9, 17, ... skips the one bound where the bug is cheap to
find. Four FALSE tasks the f53bf26d baseline answered in 0.4 to 14 s time out
at 60 s under step 8:

| task | bound that finds it | solve at the next bounds |
|---|---|---|
| list-ext2-properties/simple_and_skiplist_2lvl-1 | 2 (0.4 s) | 3 s at 3, timeout from 4 |
| list-ext2-properties/list_and_tree_cnstr-1 | 2 (0.9 s) | timeout from 3 |
| ldv-sets/test_mutex_unbounded-2 | 2 (5.6 s) | timeout from 3 |
| memory-model/2SB | 10 (14 s) | timeout from 11 |

No single step recovers all four (step 2 reaches skiplist at bound 3;
doubling misses 2SB's 10); only going one bound at a time through the small
bounds does. The choice here is to follow ESBMC's wrapper rather than tune
the schedule on one benchmark; a schedule that starts at step 1 and widens
remains an option once a full ReachSafety rerun can weigh it.
